This document has two parts: a word-for-word video script (≤3 minutes), and ready-to-send LinkedIn DM scripts for each target contact.

## Part 1 — Video Script (≤3 min)

Record this as a Loom or unlisted YouTube video. Keep the camera on. Show the dashboard on screen from Step 3 onward. Aim for 2:45.

---

**[0:00–0:15] The hook — say this to camera, no screen share yet**

"In January I applied for Quidax's graduate trainee programme. I didn't get in. So I spent the next few months building two production tools for Quidax anyway — unprompted. This is the second one. It's called PulseDesk, and it's running live on your Telegram community right now."

---

**[0:15–0:45] The problem — stay on camera or show a busy Telegram group**

"Quidax runs one of the most active crypto communities in West Africa. Thousands of messages a day. Support agents have to scroll through all of it manually — greetings, price talk, memes — to find the actual issues. That means someone's withdrawal that's been stuck since morning gets buried.

I built a tool that reads every message automatically, picks out the real support issues, and prioritizes them. While keeping a human in control of every decision."

---

**[0:45–1:30] The dashboard — switch to screen share**

*Show the main ticket feed.*
"Here's the live view. Every card is a real message from your community, classified by AI. Notice the urgency tags — that's prioritization the team doesn't have to do manually.

Click into one ticket.
"Inside a ticket: the original message, the AI's category — 'Withdrawal Issue, High' — a short summary, and a suggested reply the agent can edit and send. Nothing is sent automatically; the human is always the last step."

*Scroll to show a Pidgin-language ticket if one is visible.*
"This one's in Nigerian Pidgin — 'money never enter since morning.' The system understands 'money never enter' means a deposit hasn't arrived, not general chatter. That took real work to get right."

---

**[1:30–2:00] Honest KPIs — point at the cards**

"The numbers at the top are deliberately honest. Resolution rate currently runs about 46% — that's tickets where the team got to a resolution the system could actually observe. An earlier version showed 80% because it was counting spam as resolutions. I fixed that.

The Median Response Time is 6.5 minutes — the 50th-percentile ticket. One outlier was skewing the mean to 8 hours, which is why I use the median.

These are the numbers I'd want if I were running the support operation."

---

**[2:00–2:25] The human loop — navigate to /train**

"The AI makes mistakes. So there's a training screen where agents review labels one at a time. Every correction feeds back into future classifications — the system gets smarter on this specific community's language over time.

And it also learns from what admins do naturally in Telegram. If an admin's reply implies a different category, the ticket is quietly corrected — no extra dashboard needed."

---

**[2:25–2:45] Close — back to camera**

"This is deployed on Railway and has been running on your live community data since mid-June. It is not a mock-up.

I'd love 15 minutes with the right person at Quidax. Link to the live dashboard is in the description — password is in my message. If you want the code: github.com/JimiR3d — this repo is there."

## Things to NOT do

- Do not trigger a live message send to the Telegram group during a demo — the account cannot post (broadcast-only group, `USER_BANNED_IN_CHANNEL`).
- Do not show the AI Accuracy Benchmark as the headline number — it is a self-graded internal check, not a third-party validation.
- Do not show the Verification panel as a live demo of accuracy — the score depends on how many real tickets have been reviewed by a human, and it can read low if the review queue is small.
- Do not run `/api/verify` live on screen — it takes 1–2 minutes of AI calls and the score may look confusing out of context.
- Do not claim the outbound reply feature is "live" — it is built and verified, but blocked by the broadcast-only group setting.
